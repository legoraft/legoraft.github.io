I read a lot of post in different technical communities, be it hacker news, reddit or github, I love lurking and reading what people think about different topics. Nobody on the internet seems to agree on anything, but a thing I see a lot of consensus on (especially within the self-host and linux community) is that you **shouldn't** be the "family IT person". I think that we need a different perspective on this.

Online I've read a lot of comments how hosting a service for someones creates an SLA[^1] you need to maintain or helping your grandma set up a laptop means you should be prepared to be called every day. Most of these comments will be based on real-life situations people have experienced, but I don't think that this is a reason to abandon being the IT person in your family.

My favorite approach to this is what Luke has said on the [WAN show](!todo). He said that being the family IT guy should be appreciated, because you stay in touch with those family members that call with their problems. Of course, if you're the IT person for your family, you will be called more often for often benign reasons, but you still can help them, often with a simple google search, which makes them happy. You also stay in touch with a lot of people way more, which is a fun way to look at it.

[^1]: A Service Level Agreement, a contract that says that you should supply certain services to the other party.
